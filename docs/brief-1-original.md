# PROJECT: BhuVerify
## A New Standard for Land Due Diligence in India

Act as an elite product designer, creative director, UX researcher, product strategist and senior full-stack engineer.

Build a premium, production-minded MVP for an Indian land due diligence platform. The product helps buyers, sellers, investors, real estate professionals, lawyers and DAO investment groups understand a property's documentation, identify potential risks, organize verification, and prepare for a future transaction.

The design must draw inspiration from the design psychology of Apple's website: editorial storytelling, exceptionally clear hierarchy, confident typography, immersive product presentation, generous whitespace, purposeful motion, minimal visual noise and an experience that makes complex technology feel effortless.

Do not copy Apple's website, assets, branding, layouts or proprietary visual elements. Interpret the underlying design principles for a distinct Indian real estate product.

The final experience should feel like a new category of premium property technology, not a conventional real estate portal, legal website or cryptocurrency dashboard.

# 1. THE CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Design around one central idea:

**Make complicated land decisions feel clear.**

Every screen must answer three questions:

1. What am I looking at?
2. What does it mean for my property?
3. What should I do next?

Follow these principles:

### Clarity over complexity
Hide unnecessary complexity until the user needs it. Present a clear summary first, with supporting evidence and detailed legal information available on demand.

### Confidence without exaggeration
Use elegant, composed language. Communicate exactly what has been checked and what remains uncertain. Never use design to make incomplete verification look conclusive.

### Progressive disclosure
Reveal information progressively. Start with the property overview, then let users explore documents, ownership history, verification findings and supporting evidence.

### Purposeful minimalism
Whitespace is functional. Typography establishes hierarchy. Every component must earn its place.

### Motion with meaning
Use animation to explain relationships, provide feedback, reveal information and establish continuity between screens. Never animate simply to impress.

### Human control
Users must be able to inspect findings, correct extracted information, revisit previous steps, save drafts and understand why a verification status changed.

# 2. BRAND AND VISUAL IDENTITY

Create a distinctive brand system for BhuVerify.

Brand personality:
- Intelligent.
- Calm.
- Precise.
- Premium.
- Trustworthy.
- Forward-looking.
- Rooted in the realities of Indian land ownership.

Avoid stereotypical real estate imagery, generic courthouse symbols, stock photos of handshakes, gold-heavy luxury branding, excessive gradients and crypto-inspired neon colors.

### Color system

Build a restrained visual palette:

- Primary background: warm white, approximately #FAFAF8.
- Main text: near-black, approximately #171717.
- Secondary text: neutral grey, approximately #777777.
- Primary action: deep blue, approximately #174EA6.
- Subtle surfaces: soft neutral grey.
- Success: muted green.
- Warning: restrained amber.
- Critical risk: accessible red.

Use color to communicate meaning, not decoration. Risk colors must always be accompanied by text labels and recognizable icons.

### Typography

Use a refined sans-serif type system such as Inter or another high-quality contemporary sans-serif.

Create an editorial hierarchy with:
- Large, confident homepage headlines.
- Short, readable supporting statements.
- Strong section titles.
- Compact labels for metadata.
- Highly legible body text.
- Monospaced typography for selected property IDs, reference numbers and audit identifiers.

Avoid making every heading oversized. Reserve dramatic typography for important storytelling moments.

### Layout

Use:
- A responsive 12-column grid on desktop.
- Generous horizontal margins.
- Consistent spacing tokens.
- Strong alignment.
- Thin, understated borders.
- Restrained corner radii.
- Minimal shadows.
- Large, clean content surfaces.
- Persistent but unobtrusive navigation.

Do not turn every section into a floating card. Use open layouts, dividers and typography to create structure.

# 4. THE APPLICATION EXPERIENCE

The signed-in application must maintain the same visual quality as the public website while prioritizing efficient professional work.

Use a refined desktop application shell with:
- A compact left sidebar.
- A restrained top bar.
- A contextual property header.
- A spacious main content area.
- Right-side contextual details only when useful.

Primary navigation:
1. Overview
2. Properties
3. Documents
4. Due Diligence
5. Reviews
6. Reports
7. Team and Access

Include a separate Transaction Readiness section, marked as an early-stage or future capability as appropriate.

## Dashboard

Headline:
"Your land portfolio."

Subheading:
"Every property, every check, every outstanding question."

Display a concise overview of:
- Properties in progress.
- Documents awaiting review.
- Unresolved high-risk findings.
- Reviews awaiting action.

Below this, show a clean property table with search, filters and recent activity.

Make the most important next action visually prominent.

Avoid excessive KPI cards, decorative charts and unnecessary dashboard metrics.

## Property overview

Create a detailed property workspace.

Header:
Property name and unique ID.

Subheader:
Village, tehsil, district, state and relevant property identifiers.

Show:
- Claimed area.
- Property type.
- Claimed owner or ownership structure.
- Document collection progress.
- Verification activity.
- Unresolved findings.
- Assigned reviewer.
- Last updated date.

Create a clear distinction between property information supplied by the user and information independently verified.

Use a restrained parcel visualization with a clear legend and an explicit indication of whether boundaries are illustrative or sourced from authoritative survey data.

## Document experience

Treat the document vault as a first-class product feature.

Use a clean document list with:
- Document type.
- File name.
- Date.
- Related parties.
- Property identifiers.
- Extraction status.
- Verification status.
- Version history.

When a document is opened, use a split-view layout with the document preview on the left and extracted information on the right.

Allow users to select an extracted field and inspect its source location when available.

Highlight possible inconsistencies without automatically labelling them fraud.

Provide clear states for processing, completion, failure and manual review.

## Due diligence experience

Headline:
"Understand what has been checked."

Group checks into:
- Ownership and title history.
- Revenue records and mutation.
- Encumbrances and third-party interests.
- Land use and planning.
- Litigation and disputes.
- Boundaries and physical inspection.
- Transaction requirements.

Each check should show its status, source, evidence, reviewer and next action.

Use progressive disclosure so that the overview remains calm while detailed evidence is available when requested.

Separate risk severity from verification completion.

A completed workflow must never automatically mean that the property has a legally clear title.

## Findings experience

Headline:
"What needs attention."

Create a clear, evidence-linked register of unresolved issues.

Each finding should contain:
- Concise title.
- Plain-language explanation.
- Severity.
- Supporting evidence.
- Source and date.
- Assigned owner.
- Recommended next step.
- Resolution status.

Example:

"Plot area differs between records"

"Two supplied documents state different areas for the same property. Confirm the correct area against the relevant authoritative records and obtain professional review before proceeding."

This is an illustrative finding, not a statement about an actual property.

Make the next action obvious. Do not use alarming language or unexplained legal terminology.

## Review and approval

Allow authorized users to:
- Assign checks.
- Request additional evidence.
- Add comments.
- Record review decisions.
- Request corrections.
- Approve internal workflow stages.
- Inspect the complete audit history.

Clearly distinguish an internal DAO approval from legal certification, government approval or registration.

# 5. THE INTERACTION AND MOTION SYSTEM

Motion should create a sense of precision and continuity.

Use:
- Smooth section transitions.
- Subtle opacity and position changes.
- Document panel transitions.
- Animated completion indicators.
- Gentle expansion of evidence details.
- Short feedback animations after saving.
- Carefully controlled hover states.
- Progressive reveal of the parcel visualization.

Use natural easing and short, restrained durations.

Avoid:
- Continuous floating animations.
- Excessive parallax.
- Constantly moving backgrounds.
- Scroll hijacking.
- Long transitions between routine tasks.
- Motion that makes document reading difficult.

Respect reduced-motion preferences. All essential information must remain accessible without animation.

# 6. MICROCOPY AND PRODUCT VOICE

Write like a premium technology company: concise, composed, precise and human.

Prefer:
- "Review documents"
- "View source"
- "Resolve finding"
- "Request missing records"
- "Ready for legal review"
- "Verification not completed"

Avoid:
- "100% safe land"
- "Fraud-proof property"
- "Guaranteed clear title"
- "AI-certified ownership"
- "Instant government verification" unless that exact integration exists and has successfully completed the check.

Use plain English, with Indian property terminology displayed where relevant. Support future localization into Hindi and other Indian languages.

# 7. INDIA-SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS

Design for the diversity of Indian land administration.

Support configurable state-specific terminology and workflows, including relevant identifiers such as Khasra, Gata, survey number, Khatauni, Record of Rights and Jamabandi, depending on jurisdiction.

Do not assume that all states use identical records, measurement units, conversion factors, land-use rules or registration procedures.

Make government-data integrations explicit and configurable.

For Uttar Pradesh, structure the application so that relevant state land-record and registration workflows can be added without redesigning the entire product.

Treat OCR extraction as preliminary until the information is appropriately verified.

Every report must disclose the scope of checks, evidence reviewed, sources consulted, limitations and unresolved questions.

Protect uploaded deeds, personal information and other sensitive documents with authentication, appropriate access controls, private storage and audit logging.

# 8. TECHNICAL EXECUTION

Build a working MVP, not a static concept.

Use a modern React-based stack with a secure backend such as Supabase or an equivalent supported service.

Implement:
- Authentication.
- Role-based access.
- Property CRUD.
- Secure document upload and retrieval.
- Persistent database storage.
- Search and filtering.
- Verification checklists.
- Risk and finding management.
- Reviewer assignment.
- Activity history.
- PDF report generation.
- Responsive layouts.
- Loading, error, empty and success states.

Use fictional demonstration records until real integrations are configured.

Never present an API integration, document extraction, government lookup, legal review or registration action as successful unless it actually completed.

Prioritize the full journey from creating a property to generating a due diligence report before building advanced integrations.

# 9. FINAL QUALITY BAR

Before considering the MVP complete, evaluate it against these questions:

- Can a first-time user understand the product within five seconds?
- Is the primary action obvious on every screen?
- Can a user distinguish supplied information from verified evidence?
- Can an unresolved issue be understood without a legal background?
- Can a lawyer inspect the underlying evidence?
- Can an investor understand what remains uncertain?
- Does the interface remain calm when the underlying information is complex?
- Does every important action produce clear feedback?
- Is sensitive information protected?
- Are unsupported claims and simulated verifications avoided?
- Does the product work at desktop, tablet and mobile sizes?

Make deliberate design decisions. Do not add features or decorative elements merely because they are fashionable.

The final result should be visually distinctive, functionally credible and ready to demonstrate to prospective customers.

The guiding principle is simple:

**The technology may be complex. The experience should not be.**
